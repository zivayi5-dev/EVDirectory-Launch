Planning EV Fleet Charging Infrastructure for UK Businesses

How to plan EV fleet charging infrastructure. Grid capacity, DNO applications, phased rollout and cost management for UK fleet operators.

EV fleet charging infrastructure planning is the critical first step for any UK business converting a vehicle fleet to electric. This guide walks through every stage — from grid capacity assessment to DNO notifications, phased rollout strategy, WCS grant applications and finding an OZEV-approved fleet EV charger installer.

Step 1: Assess Your Grid Connection for Fleet EV Charging

Contact your Distribution Network Operator (DNO) to understand your current grid capacity. For a 20-vehicle fleet with 7kW chargers running simultaneously, you need at least 140kW of available capacity — which most commercial sites can accommodate with smart load balancing. Your OZEV-approved installer will carry out a free power assessment as part of the site survey.

Step 2: DNO Notification for Fleet EV Charger Installation

dno notification for fleet ev charger installation uk — grid capacity assessment

Any commercial EV charger installation above 3.68kW must be notified to your DNO. For fleet depot installations — typically involving multiple 7kW or 22kW chargers — your OZEV-approved installer submits the formal DNO notification as part of the installation process. For larger installations requiring grid capacity upgrades, a formal grid connection assessment may be needed, which can take 4–12 weeks. All installers in our directory are experienced in managing DNO notifications.

Step 3: Choosing the Right Charger for Your Fleet Depot

fleet ev charger installation uk — ozev-approved depot charging infrastructure

7kW AC chargers are ideal for overnight fleet charging. 22kW AC chargers are better for shift-based operations with daytime downtime. Rapid DC chargers (50kW–150kW) suit high-turnover logistics depots. Smart chargers with load balancing firmware are essential for multi-unit fleet installations — ensuring available power is shared efficiently across all vehicles.

Step 4: Phased Rollout — How Many Chargers to Start With

Most fleet operators start with chargers for 20–30% of the fleet and scale up as EV adoption increases. A phased approach reduces upfront capital expenditure and allows your infrastructure to grow with the fleet. The WCS grant applies to each installation phase independently — meaning you can claim up to £500/socket per phase, up to 40 sockets per site.

Step 5: WCS Grants for Fleet EV Charger Installation

Fleet operators can claim the Workplace Charging Scheme grant for up to 40 sockets per site — with each grant worth up to £500 per socket. For a fleet depot installing 20 chargers, this represents up to £10,000 in grant funding deducted directly from your installation invoice. Multiple sites can apply independently. Your OZEV-approved fleet EV charger installer manages the entire WCS application on your behalf.
